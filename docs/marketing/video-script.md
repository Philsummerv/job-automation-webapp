# The tutorial video — script and shot list

For recording **Mon 2026-08-25**. Every label and button below is checked
against the live app, so you can follow it straight through without stopping to
find things.

**Target length: 3 minutes.** Not 10. Someone in a Facebook group will give you
about twenty seconds before deciding, and a long video reads as a sales pitch.
If it runs to 4, that's fine; if you're at 7, cut the settings section.

---

## Before you hit record — five minutes of setup

1. **Make a fresh demo account.** Do not record your real log. Your actual
   claim history is your employers, your rejections, and your job titles, and
   this video is going to be public forever. Sign up with a second email and
   fill it with obviously fake employers.
2. **Pre-load 3 activities into the demo account** dated earlier in the week,
   so the dashboard isn't empty when you open it and you can demo the export
   without adding four things on camera. Leave the week one short of target —
   you want the counter to read amber, e.g. `2/3 this week`, so you can show it
   turn green.
3. **Have a screenshot ready on the desktop** to attach as evidence. Any image.
4. **Browser prep:** new window, no other tabs, no bookmarks bar, zoom to 125%
   so the text is readable on a phone. Close anything that pops notifications.
5. **Name it in Settings** — put a plausible fake name in *Full name*, because
   it prints on the export header and you'll be showing that.

**Recording:** OBS or the Xbox Game Bar (`Win+G`) both work and are free. Record
the browser window, not the whole desktop. 1080p.

**Face or no face:** voice over the screen is fine and faster to record. Say
your first name out loud in the intro either way — the whole pitch is that a
real person built this, and a faceless voice with no name undercuts it.

**If you flub a line, don't restart.** Stop, pause two seconds, say it again.
You'll cut the pause later, and if you never edit it, nobody minds — it reads as
a real person, which is the point.

## The script

Speak it, don't read it. These are the beats, in your words.

### 0:00–0:25 — Who you are and why this exists

> Hey, I'm [name]. I've been on unemployment in New York since March, and every
> week you have to certify that you looked for work. I never missed a week — I
> just spent a lot of time wanting to be sure I hadn't, because I kept losing
> track of what I'd actually done. So I built this to keep the record for me,
> and I'm going to show you how it works. It takes about three minutes.

**On screen:** the landing page at jobassistui.com, not scrolling, just sitting
there.

Do not open with the product name or a feature list. The reason anyone watches
past ten seconds is that you're one of them.

### 0:25–0:45 — Signing in

**On screen:** click **Sign in** → type the demo email → the page that says
*"We'll email you a sign-in code — no password needed."*

> You put in your email and it sends you a six-digit code. There's no password
> to make up or forget.

**Cut the wait.** Stop recording while the mail arrives, restart with the code
going in. Nobody needs to watch an inbox.

### 0:45–1:15 — Settings (the part that makes the rest make sense)

**On screen:** Settings. Point at each field as you say it.

> First time in, you tell it three things. Your state. **Weekly activity
> requirement** — how many activities you need each week, most states are three
> to five, and yours is on your state's own paperwork. And **Reporting week
> starts on** — mine's Sunday because that's when New York certifies. That last
> one matters, because it's what makes the weekly count line up with the week
> your state actually asks about.

Say this next line as written, near enough:

> Set these to what your state tells you. I can't set them for you — I only
> know New York, and the requirements are different everywhere.

That sentence is doing real work. It's the honest version, and it's also what
stops someone taking a number off your video and getting it wrong.

### 1:15–2:00 — Logging an activity (the core of the video)

**On screen:** **+ Add activity**. Fill it in slowly enough to follow.

> Every time you apply somewhere, you add it here. Date. **Method** — online
> application, in person, phone, email, job fair, networking. Who it was with.
> Job title and the listing link if you have them, both optional. Then the
> **Result** — applied, interviewed, callback, no response, whatever it turned
> into. You can come back and change that later when they get back to you.
>
> And this is the one I'd actually use: **Evidence screenshot.** Attach the
> confirmation page. If anybody ever asks you to prove it, that's the thing
> you'll want and it's the thing nobody keeps.

Submit. Land back on the log.

> That's it. That's the whole loop — about fifteen seconds each time, right
> after you apply, while you still remember it.

**Point at the counter changing to green `3/3 this week`.**

> And that tells you where you are for the week. That's the part I built it
> for, honestly. Sunday night, not guessing.

### 2:00–2:30 — The export

**On screen:** **Export PDF** on the week header. Open the PDF, scroll it once.

> Any week, you can pull down a PDF or a CSV — every activity with the date,
> employer, method, and result, with your name on it.

Then say this, and don't soften it:

> I want to be straight about what this is: it's a clean copy of what you did,
> that you can print if you ever need it. I'm not telling you your state accepts
> this as a form, because I can't promise that for fifty states and nobody
> should. It's your record, kept properly.

That paragraph is the most important thirty seconds in the video. It's the
honest claim, and it's also the answer to the first skeptical comment you'll
get.

### 2:30–3:00 — Price, and the ask

> It's free. Not a trial, no card, nothing to cancel — free for everyone until
> January 1st. I'm on unemployment myself and I can't take income before then
> anyway. After that it's twelve dollars a month, and you can decide then.
>
> It's new and I built it on my own, so there are going to be things wrong with
> it. There's a Feedback link inside the app and it comes straight to my inbox.
> If something breaks or something's confusing, that's the most useful thing you
> could send me. Link's below. Thanks for watching.

**Last frame:** the dashboard, sitting still. Not a logo, not an outro card.

## Publishing it

- **Title:** plain and searched-for, not clever.
  `How I track my weekly work search for unemployment (free tool I built)`
- **Description:** two sentences of the same story, then the link, then one line:
  *"This is a record-keeping tool. It isn't legal advice and requirements vary
  by state — check with your own state's office."*
- **Unlisted or public?** **Public.** Unlisted can't be found by search, and
  YouTube search is a slow channel that keeps working after you stop posting —
  same logic as the SEO pages in phase 5.
- **No thumbnail with arrows and a shocked face.** A plain frame of the app, or
  none. The audience is people worried about their benefits, and a hype
  thumbnail reads as a scam to exactly them.
- **Turn comments on** and answer them, including the hostile ones — same rule
  as the group posts.

## Where the video actually goes — read this before you post it

**Do not lead with the video in a Facebook group.** A post whose payload is a
YouTube link gets throttled by Facebook (off-platform video is exactly what its
ranking suppresses) and reads as an ad, which is the one thing the whole
playbook is built to avoid. The plain-text post stays the plain-text post.

The video's real jobs, in order of value:

1. **The answer to "is this a scam."** When someone asks in a comment — and they
   will — replying with a three-minute video of a guy showing his screen settles
   it in a way no paragraph does.
2. **The thing you send an admin.** It makes the ask-permission DM far easier to
   say yes to, because they can see exactly what they'd be letting in.
3. **The job centers, phase 4.** If a workshop coordinator says "show me," this
   is what you send. If they let you speak for five minutes, this *is* the five
   minutes.
4. **A link on the landing page**, later, once you've watched it back and still
   like it.
5. **YouTube search itself**, slowly, for "unemployment work search log" type
   queries.

So: record it today, put it up, and keep posting plain text. The video is
ammunition for conversations you're already in, not a new channel.
