# Admin DM drafts

One section per group. Each section says whether it was sent. Unsent entries are
drafts for the owner to review, edit, and send himself.

The general shape that works, and why:

1. **Name their promo rule in the first two lines.** This is the whole trick. It
   proves you read the rules and chose to ask, which is the exact opposite of
   what a spammer does.
2. **Lead with being on unemployment yourself**, not with the product.
3. **Say plainly it's free** — no card, no payment, no income until January.
4. **No link.** You're asking permission, not sneaking a preview.
5. **Make "no" easy**, and promise to keep helping in the comments either way.
   A refusal then costs you nothing and you stay welcome in the group.

Keep them short. Admins are volunteers reading this on a phone.

**Draft — copy everything between the lines, paste straight into Messenger.**
No bold markers or quote marks: Messenger shows `**` as literal asterisks, so

```text
Hi Sydnee — I'm a member of the group. I'm in New York and I've been claiming unemployment myself since March.

This is a heads up: I'm planning to post about something I built, and I saw rule 4, so I wanted to ask your permission first rather than just putting it up and hoping.

What it is: I kept messing up my own work search log — forgetting which day I applied where, then scrambling on Sunday trying to remember three activities before I certified. The money, up front, since that's what rule 4 is about: it's completely free until January 1st — no card, no trial, nothing to enter. That's not a marketing thing; I'm on unemployment myself and I can't take income before then. On January 1st it becomes $12 a month, and that includes people who signed up early. I'm not going to tell your group it's free and quietly change that later, so I'd rather you hear it from me now and decide with the full picture.

Why I want to post: it's new and there are probably things wrong with it. I need real people using it and telling me what's broken, and this group is full of people dealing with exactly the problem it solves.

If you're alright with it, I'd keep it to one plain post, no graphics, and I'm happy to send you the exact wording first so you can see precisely what it would say. If you'd rather I didn't post at all, that's genuinely fine — I'll keep answering questions in the comments either way, which I've been doing anyway.

Either way, thanks for running the group. Let me know what you'd prefer.
```

**Why it's built this way:**

- **It's a heads up, not a hesitant ask.** He states plainly that he intends to
  post, then asks permission because rule 4 exists. That reads as someone
  being straight with her, not someone testing the water.
- **Names rule 4 in the second paragraph.** Proves he read the rules and chose
  to ask, which is the exact opposite of what a spammer does.
- **Says what the product actually does.** She's deciding whether to put
  something in front of 4,700 people; "a free website" tells her nothing.
- **Names the three-a-week requirement**, which signals he knows the actual NY
  rules rather than just that unemployment exists.
- **Details the paywall in its own labelled paragraph**, including that early
  signups are not grandfathered. Rule 4 exists because people lie about exactly
  this. Volunteering it is what makes everything else believable, and her
  finding out later would poison the group's trust in him permanently.
- **Offers to show her the post text first.** Costs her nothing, gives her
  control, and turns a yes/no into a conversation.
- **Ends by making "no" free**, and by thanking her for running the group —
  which is unpaid work almost nobody acknowledges.

**Shorter version**, if she seems like someone who wants it brief: cut the "why
I want to post" paragraph and the offer to preview the wording. Keep the heads
up, keep rule 4, keep what it does, keep the paywall paragraph intact — that
one never gets cut.

**If she says yes:** ask whether she'd rather post it herself. An admin-posted
or pinned recommendation is worth ten of yours.

**If she says no:** thank her, stay in the group, keep answering questions. The
reply play works without ever posting a link, and in this group it's the
stronger play anyway given how few comments there are.

**If she doesn't reply:** DMs from non-friends land in Message Requests and can
sit for days. Don't chase it. Keep commenting — that's what makes your name
familiar when she does read it.

not a second pitch. No link, no new argument, no ask beyond the one already
made.
