# The Facebook playbook — getting the first JobAssistUI users

Channel #1 of the plan agreed 2026-08-19. Facebook state unemployment groups
first, because they are the largest, most permissive gathering of exactly the
people this is for, and the age demographic actually uses Facebook.

**The pitch is the true one**, everywhere below: *I'm job searching too. I built
the thing I wanted. It's free until Jan 2027 because I can't take income before
then. Tell me what breaks.* That survives in communities that auto-delete SaaS
promos, because it isn't one.

---

## Before you post anywhere: your profile

People will click your name before they click your link. If your profile looks
empty or brand-new, you read as a bot and the post dies — or gets reported.

- Use your **real personal profile**. A fresh one made for this is worse, not
  better: no history is the exact signature of a spam account.
- Make sure a stranger clicking your name sees a real photo and some ordinary
  life.
- **Do not create a Page and post as the Page.** Most groups block Page posts,
  and members distrust them. You're posting as a guy who built a thing, because
  that's what's true.

## Day 1 — Find and join (about an hour)

Search Facebook, then filter to **Groups**:

```
[State] Unemployment
[State] Unemployment Help
[State] EDD                    (California — this one is huge)
Unemployment Benefits [State]
[State] Unemployment Questions
```

Start with high-population states: **CA, TX, FL, NY, PA, GA, OH, MI, NJ, IL**.

For each group, check three things before joining:
- **Members** — 5,000+ is worth your time
- **Activity** — the group info shows posts per day; you want double digits
- **Rules** — click "About," read them now, note whether self-promo is banned

**Join 6–8 groups. Private groups beat public ones** — public groups are drowning
in spam and their members are jaded; private ones have more trust and more real
conversation.

Many will ask join questions like "are you currently claiming benefits?" Answer
honestly and briefly. **Do not mention your website in the join questions** —
that's an instant reject.

## Day 1, before you close the tab — capture what you'll need

Two things, both cheap now and expensive later.

1. **Fill in `groups.md`** (next to this file) while each group's About page is
   still open: member count, what the Rules tab says about promotion, and the
   name of at least one admin. Day 4 and Day 5 both read off that table.
2. **Look at your own profile the way a stranger will.** Your profile → the
   three dots → **View As**. An admin vets you in about eight seconds: photo,
   some ordinary history, not obviously an account made last week.

## Days 2–4 — Be a member, not a marketer

This part is not optional, and it's where people skip ahead and get banned.

**Fifteen minutes a day, split in two passes** — one in the morning, one in the
evening. These groups are busiest when people are certifying and when they get
bad news, which is most of the day.

**Comment on 2–3 posts a day.** What to answer:

- Mechanics questions you genuinely know — what counts as an activity, whether
  contacts count, what to keep as proof, how to record something.
- Anything where the honest answer is "here's how I keep track of mine",
  described in words, with no link.
- **Say when you don't know**, and say it plainly. "I'd call the office on that
  one, I don't want to guess about your money" earns more trust than an answer,
  and it is the correct thing to say about somebody's benefits.

What to stay out of:

- Political threads and rants about the state agency. There is no version of
  that where you come out ahead.
- Arguments already in progress.
- Anything where being right matters more to you than the person being helped.

**Post no links. Mention your site zero times.** Not once, not in a comment, not
in a DM to someone who seemed interested.

**Save the recurring questions.** Facebook's Save feature, or paste them in a
file. The questions that come up over and over are, in order: your canned
answers, your FAQ page, and eventually your per-state SEO pages. Three days of
reading these groups is the cheapest customer research you will ever do.

Two reasons this matters. Group members can see you're a real participant. And
Facebook's own spam detection treats "joined 4 hours ago, posted an external
link" as textbook spam behavior — it can shadow-limit the post before a human
ever sees it.

## Day 4 — Message the admins (the highest-leverage move)

For each group where you want to post, find an admin in the member list and send
a short message:

> Hi — I'm a member here and I'm on unemployment myself. I built a free website
> for tracking work-search activities for the weekly claim, because I kept
> messing up my own spreadsheet. It's free, there's no payment in it at all, and
> I'm mostly looking for people to tell me what's broken. Is it alright if I post
> about it, or would you rather I didn't? Happy either way, just didn't want to
> break the rules.

Admins say yes more often than you'd expect, because it's genuinely useful to
their members and you asked. And if they say no, you've lost nothing — instead of
being banned.

**If an admin says no:** thank them, stay in the group, and keep answering
questions. You can still help people one-to-one when someone posts a problem your
tool solves (see "The reply play" below), which is where most of your early users
will actually come from anyway.

**If an admin says yes:** ask whether they'd like you to post it, or whether
they'd rather post it themselves. An admin-posted or pinned recommendation is
worth ten of yours.

## Day 5 — The post

One post per group, spaced out — **not all on the same day**. Posting the same
link into six groups within an hour is the pattern Facebook flags.

Write it in your own words. The shape that works:

> I've been on unemployment since [month] and I kept screwing up my work search
> log — forgetting which day I applied where, then panicking on Sunday trying to
> remember three activities.
>
> So I built a website to keep track of it. You add each application or contact
> as you do it, it tells you where you are for the week, and you can print out a
> clean record if you ever have to show one.
>
> It's free. Not a trial, not a card — I'm on unemployment myself and I can't
> take income until January anyway. It's new so there are probably things wrong
> with it, and honestly that's why I'm posting: I want people to tell me what
> breaks.
>
> [link]
>
> Happy to answer anything about it.

**Post on a weekday morning**, roughly 9–11am local. Weekend posts get buried
under the certification-day traffic.

Rules for the post itself:
- **No graphics, no logo, no marketing formatting.** Plain text like a person
  typing. Anything that looks designed reads as an ad.
- **Never claim your state accepts the export**, or that this protects their
  benefits. Nobody can warrant that for 50 states, and it's the fastest way to
  deserve a complaint. "A clean record you can print" is the honest version.
- **Answer every comment**, including the hostile ones. Someone saying "this is
  a scam to get your info" is doing you a favor — answer them calmly and in
  public, and other readers trust you more than if nobody had asked.
- Do not delete critical comments.

**Stay on the post for the first hour.** Early comments decide whether Facebook
shows it to anyone else, and a post the author abandoned reads as a drive-by ad.
Answering three comments in the first hour is worth more than anything else you
do that day.

## The reply play (works even where you can't post)

This produces better users than the posts do. Every day, someone in these groups
posts a version of:

> "How many job searches do I need this week?"
> "Do I have to write down where I applied?"
> "They're auditing my work search and I don't remember what I did"

Answer the question **first and completely**, with no link. Then, only if it's
actually relevant, one line: "I built a free thing that tracks this if it's
useful — [link] — no payment, I'm on unemployment too." If the group forbids
links, leave it out and let the profile do the work.

## Cadence, once you're going

- 15–20 min/day in the groups. Comment, answer, be present.
- One new group joined per week.
- Never more than one promotional post per group per month.

## What to measure

You already have the dashboard. Check **/admin** after posting:

- **Signups** — did the post move the number at all?
- **The funnel gap between *Onboarded* and *Logged ≥1 activity*** — this is the
  number that matters. People who made an account and got nothing out of it are
  telling you something is broken or confusing.
- **/feedback inbox** on the same page — read every one.

If ten people sign up and one logs an activity, stop posting and fix the product.
More traffic doesn't fix that; it just wastes the audience.

## Where this goes next

Channels 2–4 of the plan, in order, once Facebook is running: Reddit state subs
(help for a week before mentioning anything), American Job Centers / WorkSource
offices with a printed flyer, then per-state SEO pages. Explicitly **not**
Product Hunt / HN / Twitter — that audience is employed developers.
