# Canned answers — the questions these groups ask over and over

Sixteen answers you can paste into a Facebook comment or a Reddit reply in
about thirty seconds. This is the file that turns the reply play from an hour
a day into five minutes.

## How to use it

1. Scan the group. Find a post that matches one of the questions below.
2. Copy the block. **Change two or three words** so it isn't identical to the
   last time you posted it. Facebook's spam detection notices repeated text
   across groups, and so do people.
3. If the post has a detail the answer doesn't cover, add one sentence about
   that detail. That sentence is what makes it read as a reply and not a macro.
4. Post it. Don't add a link unless the answer says you can and the group's
   rules allow links.

The answers are written to be **useful on their own**. If the person never
clicks anything, they still got a real answer. That is the whole point — it is
also why they work.

## The rules that don't bend

- **Never tell anyone whether they qualify, how much they get, or whether
  their claim is right.** Getting that wrong costs a stranger money. Point at
  the state's own page and their phone number instead. Several answers below do
  nothing but that, on purpose.
- **Never say a state accepts the export**, or that any of this protects
  somebody's benefits.
- **Only three of the sixteen mention the site** (Q11, Q12, Q16), and each one
  only as an optional last line. Don't add mentions to the others. The ratio is
  what keeps you welcome.
- Never use two tool mentions in the same thread.
- **No emoji, no exclamation marks, no bold, no bullet lists.** These are typed
  into a comment box by a person.

## Before you use this file the first time

Two things to check once, then never again:

- **The New York work search page** is `dol.ny.gov/services/ui/wsr`. Open it and
  confirm it still loads. Several answers send people there.
- **The claims phone number.** It is deliberately not written into these
  answers. A wrong phone number pasted sixteen times is worse than no phone
  number, and the number on somebody's own paperwork is right for their claim.
  Every answer says "the number on your paperwork and on dol.ny.gov" instead.

## Other states

Answers marked **[NY]** name New York specifics and need editing before you use
them elsewhere. Answers marked **[any]** work in any state as written, because
they route the person to their own state's rules rather than stating one.

To adapt an **[NY]** answer for another state: swap `dol.ny.gov/services/ui/wsr`
for that state's own work search page, delete "Mine in New York is three a
week", and delete any sentence describing your own week. **Do not replace a New
York number with another state's number you found on a forum.** If you can't
find the state's own page in a minute, use the **[any]** version of the answer
and tell them to call.

## Q1 — "how many job searches do i need a week" [NY]

Also appears as: "is it 3 a week or 5", "how many job searches do i need",
"how many work searches for ny".

```text
It's set by your state, and it's on your own claim paperwork rather than being the same number for everybody. Mine in New York is three a week, but I wouldn't take my number for yours. New York's page is dol.ny.gov/services/ui/wsr and other states have their own version of it. If your paperwork isn't clear, call and ask before the week ends rather than after.
```

## Q2 — "do i actually have to write down where i applied" [any]

Also appears as: "does anyone even check the work search", "do they ask for
proof", "do i need to keep a log".

```text
Every state I've looked at expects you to be able to produce a record if they ask, even when they don't collect it every week. Nobody checks most weeks, which is exactly why people get caught out on the week somebody does. Employer, date, how you applied and what came of it covers most of what gets asked for. I'd keep it somewhere that isn't just your memory.
```

## Q3 — "theyre auditing my work search and i dont remember what i did" [any]

Also appears as: "they asked me for my work search records", "i got a
questionnaire about my job searches".

**No link on this one, ever.** Somebody who has just been audited is frightened
about money, and a link there reads as taking advantage. Help and stop.

```text
That's a horrible letter to open, sorry. For rebuilding it: your email confirmations, your sent folder, the applied history on Indeed or LinkedIn or wherever you applied, and your browser history will get you most of the way there. Write down what you can actually back up and don't fill the gaps with guesses. On the audit itself, call the office and ask what they specifically want from you — I don't want to guess about your money.
```

## Q4 — "what counts as a work search activity" [NY]

Also appears as: "does applying on indeed count", "does a job fair count",
"what counts as a job search".

```text
It's usually more than just submitting applications. Interviews, job fairs, contacting an employer directly, and some career center workshops or appointments count in a lot of states. What counts is set by your state though, so go off their list and not mine — New York's is at dol.ny.gov/services/ui/wsr. If something you did is borderline, log it with a note about what it was and ask them, rather than dropping it.
```

## Q5 — "does it count if they never got back to me" [any]

Also appears as: "does it count if i didnt hear anything", "what if the job was
already filled".

```text
As far as I know the activity is the applying, not whether anybody replies. If it worked the other way round nobody would ever meet the requirement. I'd still record the ones that went nowhere, with the date and where you sent it. If your state's wording is unclear on it, that's one phone call worth making so you stop wondering about it every week.
```

## Q6 — "my payment says pending what does that mean" [any]

Also appears as: "my claim says pending", "why is my payment still pending",
"pending for 2 weeks now".

This is the most common question in these groups and the honest answer is that
you don't know. Say that. It earns more trust than a guess.

```text
I don't know, and I'd rather say that than guess — pending covers a lot of different situations and some of them clear on their own. If it's been sitting more than a week I'd call rather than wait it out. Use the claims number on your paperwork, or the one on dol.ny.gov, and call right when they open. That's the only thing that's ever worked for me.
```

## Q8 — "how long till i actually get paid" [any]

Also appears as: "when does the money hit", "how long did yours take",
"first payment taking forever".

```text
Mine has been steady once it started, but the first payment took a lot longer than any of the ones after it, and it varies with whatever's on your claim. If it's your first one, slow is normal and worth waiting out a bit. If it's a payment that used to arrive and has stopped, that's the one I'd call about rather than wait on.
```

## Q10 — "nobody is calling me back is it me" [any]

Also appears as: "50 applications and nothing", "is anyone else getting no
responses", "im starting to feel like theres no point".

```text
It's mostly not you. I've had stretches of a dozen applications with nothing at all, then two replies in the same day for no reason I could work out. The thing that helped me was counting what I sent instead of counting what came back, because the sending is the only half of it I actually control. Keep the number up and let the rest happen.
```

## Q11 — "how do you all keep track of your job searches" [any]

Also appears as: "whats the best way to keep track", "what do you use to log
your work search", "spreadsheet or app".

This one is the bullseye. Answer it properly first.

```text
Whatever you use, write it down the same day. The note takes fifteen seconds while you're still on the confirmation page, and rebuilding a week from memory takes an hour and you still won't trust it. I'd record employer, date, how you applied and what came of it, and screenshot the confirmation while it's on screen. A spreadsheet does the job fine — mine worked until the week I forgot to open it.
```

## Q13 — "can i work part time and still claim" [any]

Also appears as: "can i do doordash while on unemployment", "will a few hours
mess up my claim".

```text
In a lot of states you can, with the payment reduced and the hours reported — but how it's calculated is state-specific and I'm not putting a number on your claim. Report the hours in the week you work them, not later, whatever the rule turns out to be. Get the actual answer from the office before you accept anything, so it isn't a surprise on a payment.
```

## Q14 — "i got an overpayment notice" [any]

Also appears as: "they say i owe them money", "overpayment for weeks i already
got paid".

```text
Read the notice for the appeal deadline first and put that date somewhere you'll see it, because that's the part that's hard to undo once it passes. Then call and ask them to explain which weeks it covers and why. I'm not going to guess at whether it's right — that one needs the office, and a lot of areas have a free legal aid line for exactly this kind of notice.
```

## Q15 — "how do i get someone on the phone" [NY]

Also appears as: "nobody ever answers", "been calling for 3 days", "does anyone
have a number that works".

```text
Call the moment they open, not later in the day — that's the only thing that's ever made a difference for me. Use the number on your own paperwork and on dol.ny.gov rather than one off a forum, because the numbers get shared around and half of them are dead. Also check your NY.gov inbox while you're waiting, since things quietly show up there and never get mentioned anywhere else.
```

## Q16 — "do i need screenshots or proof of applying" [any]

Also appears as: "what proof do i need", "should i be saving confirmation

```text
Nobody's ever asked me for one. The week they ask is the week you'll wish you had them. I grab the confirmation page after I hit submit, or the confirmation email if there is one, and keep it with the date. It takes a few seconds and it's the thing that turns "I applied" into something you can actually show somebody.
```

## What to do when the post doesn't match any of these

Draft it. `reply-brief.md` is the file you paste into a Claude chat, then paste
the post underneath it. You get a draft with no link and, where it's warranted,
one with.

And if the honest answer is "I don't know," post that. It is a real answer, it
is the correct one about somebody's benefits, and in these groups it earns more
than a confident wrong one.
