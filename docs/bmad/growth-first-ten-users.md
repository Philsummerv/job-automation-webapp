# First ten users — seven days, under 30 minutes a day

**Written 2026-09-02 by Dana (Growth). Starts Thursday 2026-09-03.**

The product is live, free, and works on a phone. It has zero users. One DM has
been sent. Nothing else has happened. This file is the shortest path from that
to ten people who have signed up and logged something.

Read `docs/marketing/canned-answers.md` before Day 1. It is the file that makes
the daily work take five minutes instead of an hour. Everything else you need is
already written in `docs/marketing/`.

---

## The assessment: is the reply play really the best first move

**Yes as the engine, no as the whole of day one, and not in the group it was
picked for.**

The reply play is right. In "New York Unemployment/Updates And Help" there were
~1,600 posts last month against 29 comments. Someone who actually answers
questions is the only one in the room. It needs no admin permission, it can't
get you banned, and each reply is visible the same day — which matters, because
work you can't see the result of is the kind you stop doing.

But there is a problem with it that nothing in `docs/marketing/` accounts for:

> **That group pre-moderates comments.** Two of your comments have been sitting
> in "Pending admin approval" since 2026-08-29. The only admin, Sydnee Gerstel,
> hasn't cleared them and hasn't answered the DM sent 2026-08-30.

In a pre-moderated group the reply play produces nothing visible at all. You'd
spend a week writing genuinely good answers into a queue nobody empties, see
zero, and conclude the channel doesn't work — when what actually happened is one
volunteer admin has been busy. So the reply play is the engine, but it has to
run in groups where your comment appears when you press post. Finding those
takes ten minutes and is Day 1.

**Two things beat it on day one, and both take fifteen minutes:**

1. **Post the founder story on your own Facebook profile, publicly.** No admin,
   no rules tab, no permission, no queue. Your friends aren't the target
   audience, but some of them know somebody who is, and a share from a friend
   carries more trust than anything you can say in a group. It is also the only
   move in this document that puts the link in front of real people on the first
   morning with certainty rather than hope.
2. **Find out which of your groups pre-moderate.** Ten minutes. It decides
   where the other six days go.

**What does not beat it:** job centers (phase 4) convert best per hour but need
a flyer, a trip, and someone else's schedule — too slow to be the first move,
though one phone call is worth making this week. Reddit is real but the
comment-karma gate means it pays out in week two, not week one. Recording the
video is not a first move either; it is ammunition for conversations you aren't
having yet.

**Be honest about the number.** Ten signups in seven days from a standing start,
with no posting permission in your best group, would be a good week rather than
an expected one. Three to eight is the realistic band. The number that actually
matters is not signups at all — it is how many of them log one activity.

## Day 1 — Thursday Sep 3 (25 minutes)

**Goal: real people see the link today, and you find out where you can speak.**

**A. The visibility test (10 min).** Open each Facebook group you're in. In each
one, comment on any post with Q1, Q2 or Q10 from `canned-answers.md`. Then
refresh the page.

- Comment is there → that group is live. Write its name down somewhere, even a
  notes app. This is the whole tracking overhead and you're allowed to skip
  `groups.md` entirely.
- "Pending admin approval" → that group is queued. It is not dead, but it gets
  no more of your time this week.

While you're in each group, look at the Rules tab once and note only one thing:
**links allowed, yes or no.** That's the only per-group fact you actually need.

**B. Post on your own profile (15 min).** Public, not friends-only. Use the
weekly-burden post from `docs/marketing/post-drafts.md` — it is already written
and it is true. Two edits before you post it:

- Change "I got laid off in September" to whatever is actually right.
- Add one line at the end: "If you know somebody who's claiming, send it to
  them. That's the only thing I'm asking."

Then leave it alone. Don't refresh it.

**How you know it worked:** you have a list of which groups let you speak, and
your post is up. That's it. Signups today would be a bonus, not the measure.

## Day 2 — Friday Sep 4 (25 minutes)

**Goal: the reply habit starts, in groups that actually publish you.**

**A. Answer three posts (15 min).** Live groups only. Straight from
`canned-answers.md`, two or three words changed each time. No links, even in
groups that allow them — you're one day in.

**B. Join two more groups (10 min).** Search Facebook, filter to Groups:
`New York Unemployment`, `NYS Unemployment Help`, `Unemployment Benefits New
York`. Also worth joining: `Pennsylvania Unemployment` and `New Jersey
Unemployment`, because the questions are close enough to New York's that your
answers still work, and the two states border you.

Answer the join questions honestly. **Do not mention the site in a join
question** — that's an instant reject.

**How you know it worked:** three comments visible, two join requests in.

## Day 3 — Saturday Sep 5 (20 minutes)

**Goal: open the second channel before you need it.**

Reddit comments are permanent and show up in Google for years, which no Facebook
comment does. But new accounts get filtered, so it has to start warming now.

Go to r/Unemployment and r/NewYorkUnemployment. Sort by New. Leave **three
comments, zero links, zero mentions of the site.** Q4, Q6 and Q11 from
`canned-answers.md` fit Reddit almost unedited — cut a sentence, Reddit prefers
shorter.

Check each sub's sidebar rules on self-promotion while you're there. Some ban
links outright; those stay reply-only forever.

**How you know it worked:** three comments, no removals. If Automod eats them,
you're karma-gated — keep commenting, it clears.

## Day 4 — Sunday Sep 6 (30 minutes) — the big one

**Certification day in New York. The groups spike and the questions get real.**
This is the highest-value half hour in the week. If you only do one day, do this
one.

**A. Answer six to eight posts (25 min).** Live Facebook groups first, Reddit
after. Most of today's questions will be Q1, Q6, Q7 and Q11 — the certification
panic ones — and they're all in the file.

**B. Today the link is allowed to appear (5 min).** In a group that allows
links, on a post that is genuinely about tracking or remembering what you did,
use the optional last line under Q11. **Once. In one group.** Not in three, not
on a post about a pending payment.

**How you know it worked:** somebody replies to one of your comments. That's the
real signal this week — a reply means you were useful, and useful is what gets
clicked.

## Day 6 — Tuesday Sep 8 (25 minutes)

**Goal: one conversation with somebody whose job is helping these people.**

**A. One phone call (10 min).** Your local public library, ask for whoever runs
the job club or career programming. Libraries beat state career centers as a
first call — no bureaucracy, and they are starved for useful content.

What to say, roughly: you're claiming unemployment yourself, you built a free
website for tracking work-search activities, and you wanted to ask whether it
would be useful to the people who come to their job club. Ask a question, don't
pitch. Both answers are good — flyers on the table, or five minutes in front of
a room, and the room is worth more.

**B. Three replies (15 min).** Same as every day.

**How you know it worked:** you spoke to a person. A no is fine; you'll have
learned how they think about it, which you can't get any other way.

## Day 7 — Wednesday Sep 9 (20 minutes)

**Goal: find out what actually happened, and decide next week off facts.**

**A. Open `/admin` (5 min).** Two numbers only:

- How many signed up.
- **How many logged at least one activity.** This is the one that matters.

**B. Read the feedback inbox (5 min).** Every message. Anything that came in is
worth more than the signup count.

**C. Write four lines at the bottom of this file (10 min):** which groups
published your comments, which question got asked most, what the first objection
was, and the two numbers from `/admin`.

### How to read the numbers

| What you see | What it means | What you do next week |
|---|---|---|
| Signups, and half of them logged something | It works. | Same seven days again, plus the groups that answered your DM. |
| Signups, almost nobody logged anything | Product problem, not a traffic problem. | **Stop posting.** Walk the site as a new user on your phone and fix what's confusing. More traffic wastes the audience. |
| Almost no signups, but people replied to your comments | Too early to judge. | Run week two. Replies before clicks is the normal order. |
| No signups and no replies to anything | Wrong rooms. | Drop the queued groups entirely, add three more states, put the time into Reddit. |

## What is most likely to go wrong

**You do the work and see nothing, so you stop.** That is the failure mode here,
not a ban and not a bad post. Pre-moderation queues, Reddit's karma filter, and
the ordinary fact that a Facebook comment gets no reaction at all — three
separate ways to spend a week producing invisible work. The whole design of Day 1
is aimed at this: find the rooms where pressing post produces something you can
see, and don't spend the week talking into the ones that don't.

The second thing, well behind it: getting impatient and dropping a link in a
group that bans them. It's one removal and it can't be undone, and the group it
would happen in is the best one you've got.
